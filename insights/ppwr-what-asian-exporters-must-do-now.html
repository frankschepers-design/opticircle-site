The EU packaging regulation is live. What Mongolian and Asian exporters must do now

Frank Schepers · 1 November 2026

For years, European packaging law was something an exporter in Ulaanbaatar, Shenzhen or Ho Chi Minh City could leave to the importer. That changed on 12 August 2026. The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2025/40, now applies across all 27 member states, and it replaces a patchwork of national rules with one regulation that runs from the design of a pack to its waste.1,2

I spent fifteen years at SUEZ and SITA on the receiving end of European packaging: the sorting lines, the bales, the material that could not be recycled however hard we tried. The regulation is, in large part, Europe deciding that the design table should carry that cost rather than the sorting plant. If you put packed goods on a ship to Europe, some of that cost is now yours.

What applies, and when

  • Since 12 August 2026: the regulation applies, including a restriction on PFAS above strict limits in food-contact packaging. Such packaging can no longer be placed on the EU market.1
  • From 2028: a harmonised EU label to help consumers sort packaging waste.1
  • From 2030: limits on empty space, restrictions on certain single-use plastic formats, reuse targets, mandatory recycled content in plastic packaging, and the rule that all packaging must be recyclable.1

The 2028 and 2030 dates sound distant. They are not, for anyone whose packaging specification takes a year to change and whose buyers sign two- and three-year supply contracts.

Who carries the duty, and why it reaches you

The regulation puts the main legal duty on the manufacturer of the packaging and on the companies that make packaged products available in the EU. Before packaging is placed on the market, the manufacturer must carry out a conformity assessment, keep technical documentation and draw up an EU declaration of conformity. Packaging must also carry identification and the manufacturer's contact details, on the pack or through a QR code or other data carrier.3

Two provisions matter most to a supplier outside Europe. Article 16 requires suppliers of packaging and packaging materials to give the manufacturer all the information and documentation needed to demonstrate conformity, in a language the manufacturer understands.3 And for extended producer responsibility, a producer established outside a member state may need an authorised representative there, depending on that country's rules.2,4

In plain terms: your European buyer has to prove that your pack complies, and it cannot do that without your data. The questions will come down the chain, in writing, with deadlines.

What your buyer will ask for

In my experience the first questionnaires are rarely elegant, but they cover the same ground:

  • A component-level bill of materials for each pack: every material, every layer, every weight.
  • Confirmation on restricted substances, starting with PFAS in anything that touches food.
  • A view on recyclability, and on how much recycled content the plastic parts contain today.
  • Who will be the legal manufacturer and producer for each market, and who registers where.

Five things to do this quarter

  1. Build the pack register. List every SKU you ship to the EU with its primary, secondary and transport packaging, materials and weights. It is unglamorous work and it is the foundation for everything else.
  2. Screen food-contact packaging for PFAS now. This is the one obligation already in force. Ask your packaging suppliers for written declarations and keep them on file.
  3. Agree roles with each buyer. Settle in writing who is the manufacturer, who is the producer for EPR, and whether you need an authorised representative in any member state.
  4. Look hard at the formats that will struggle in 2030. Multi-material laminates, black plastics and oversized cartons are the usual suspects. A redesign started in 2027 is a project; one started in 2029 is a crisis.
  5. Put one person in charge of the data. Packaging compliance fails in the gap between procurement, quality and sales. One owner, one file, one answer to the buyer.

Why this is an opportunity

Exporters who answer these questions quickly and cleanly will be the suppliers that European buyers keep. I have watched the same shift in recycling: the operators who understood their material earned the long contracts. Packaging data is becoming part of what you sell.
