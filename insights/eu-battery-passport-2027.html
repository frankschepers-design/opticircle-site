EU battery passport: what changes on 18 February 2027, and what to do this quarter

Frank Schepers · 31 October 2026

From 18 February 2027, every light means of transport (LMT) battery, every electric vehicle battery and every industrial battery above 2 kWh placed on the EU market or put into service needs an electronic battery passport. That is Article 77 of the EU Batteries Regulation, and the date does not move.

Who carries the duty

The economic operator that places the finished battery on the EU market is responsible for the passport and must register it in the EU's Digital Product Passport Registry, which opened for testing on 20 July 2026. A non-EU manufacturer can create and update the passport on that operator's behalf, with written authorisation, but the legal responsibility stays with the company placing the battery on the market. For a product imported with a working battery inside, that is normally the importer.

The passport attaches to the finished battery, not to each cell or module. Where packs share a battery management system and work as one battery, the whole system needs one passport, and its combined capacity decides whether the 2 kWh threshold is crossed.

What is not needed yet

Carbon footprint, recycled content and due diligence information are not required in February 2027; each has its own later date. The public due diligence report follows from August 2027. Dynamic data, such as the battery's status and its performance and durability values, is expected from the start.

What to do this quarter

  • Decide who in your chain is the responsible economic operator for each battery model.
  • Download the Commission's list of battery passport data points and mark, field by field, who holds the data today.
  • Write to suppliers now. Material composition must be specific; the Commission says a general chemistry label such as "LFP" is not enough.
  • Test the Registry before the rush, and decide whether you will register by API, upload or by hand.
