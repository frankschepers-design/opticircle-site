Mongolia's MSE ESG report: getting the April 2027 filing right, waste data first

Frank Schepers · 1 November 2026

Six months from now, on 30 April 2027, every Tier I issuer on the Mongolian Stock Exchange must file its ESG report for the 2026 financial year on the exchange's template. Since an amendment to the disclosure rules took effect on 4 October 2024, this has been mandatory for Tier I; Tier II and III issuers may report voluntarily by the same date. The exchange has also said that Tier I issuers must publish the information in English.1,2

The second mandatory round is the one where boards, banks and foreign investors start to compare. Here is how I would approach it, and why I would start with waste.

What the first round taught us

An independent study published in October 2026 looked at every report on the exchange's public registry. Its findings are sobering. First reports produced because of the mandate had a median length of seven pages, and only 38% were posted on time. About half of all the documents filed were scanned images with no usable text, and only six of 99 documents were in English, despite the exchange describing English as part of the Tier I requirement.1

None of that is a criticism of the people who did the work. Most were filling in a template for the first time, late in the year, with data that sat in a dozen places. But a thin, late, scanned report is now visible to anyone who looks, and the people who look are the ones who lend you money and buy your products.

What the template asks for

The MSE disclosure rules set out five blocks: how sustainability is managed; environmental indicators, including energy, greenhouse gases, water, waste and biodiversity; social indicators such as labour relations and safety; governance indicators such as anti-corruption and compliance; and sector indicators for banking, agriculture, manufacturing, construction and mining. Where a company cannot report an item, it must explain the steps it is taking to disclose it in future. The report may be reviewed by an external professional organisation.3

Why start with waste

I have spent most of my career on the waste side of industry, at SUEZ and SITA in Europe and later running a sorting plant in Ghent. Waste is where I would start an ESG data system, for three practical reasons.

  • The evidence already exists. Every load that leaves your site generates a weighbridge ticket, a contractor invoice or a transfer note. Unlike many social indicators, waste can be built from documents you already hold.
  • It reaches the rest of the report. Getting waste data right forces you to map your sites, your contractors and your material flows. That map is the same one you need for energy, water and emissions.
  • It is where the money is. In every waste audit I have done, the data shows costs that nobody was tracking and materials that could be recovered. A good waste number is a management tool before it is a disclosure.

A plan from now to 30 April

  1. November: name one owner for the report, agree the data calendar with finance, and list every site and every waste contractor.
  2. December: collect twelve months of waste records by stream (hazardous and non-hazardous, and where it went: recycling, landfill, other). Note clearly what is measured and what is estimated.
  3. January: close the 2026 year and extend the same method to energy, water and workforce data.
  4. February: draft the template in English, answering every indicator. For any gap, write the reason and the plan, as the rules require.
  5. March: internal review, board sign-off, and if you choose, an external review. File a searchable PDF, not a scan.
  6. April: file early. A report posted on the 20th is a statement about how you run the company.

Looking beyond 2027

UNDP's 2026 roadmap for IFRS sustainability standards in Mongolia points towards broader and stricter reporting in the years ahead.4 A data system built properly now will carry forward; a report assembled in a hurry each April will have to be rebuilt every time the rules tighten.
